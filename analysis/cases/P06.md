## 6. Recommendation
**Run a real two-way auction, but a stabilise one:** get both down together, end with the supplier that is cheaper
*including* the relationship charge, don't push either below roughly should-cost.
- **Tarsevi:** offer **14-day payment + lead 56 + 0 tooling** for price. Ask for its **cost breakdown** (open book, `M2`)
  and negotiate on its elements. Volume is fixed, so "more volume" is the HX series itself: new work for idle shifts.
  Don't threaten. It can't afford to lose margin, so the cash it gets has to carry the price.
- **Velkenreith:** respect and partnership (its 2022 gesture), VDA A, short lead 49. Trade **60–90 d** (it isn't
  cash-short, so long payment is cheap for it) and **0 tooling** for price. Cite Tarsevi's best offer once.
- Order of asks: (1) **tooling 0**, (2) price, (3) payment days in the direction each supplier wants, (4) lead ≤ 56.
- **Target:** ≤ €200 net, 0 tooling, lead ≤ 56. **Floor:** any valid deal (no deal = 0). By the last rounds, accept
  the cheaper standing sheet (C incl. relationship) rather than risk both walking.

## What generalises (hidden RFQ / stabilise cases)
- **No alternative = no-deal score 0.** Never let both threads die; accept the best standing sheet before the round limit.
- **In stabilise / secure_supply cases with a cash-short supplier, fast payment can beat a lower price** once the price is
  near its cost floor: compare C with the relationship charge (needs a full-cost estimate), not price alone.
- In an RFQ, compare bidders on **C**, not price: lead days, payment and tooling move C by €5–20k each here.
