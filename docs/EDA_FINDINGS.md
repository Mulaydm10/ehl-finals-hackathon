# Tacto data package: deep-dive EDA

What in the data moves a negotiation, and which levers our agent should pull. Scripts:
`src/datasets/build_abc.py` (datasets A/B/C) and `src/eda/` (the analyses below). Data: package v1,
as of 2026-09-30, plus the 19 practice case files from `GET /v1/cases?set=practice`
(`datasets/cases_practice.json`).

> The data is **synthetic and templated**. That's good news: the patterns are regular and learnable,
> and the hidden final cases are generated by the same process on other suppliers.

**Takeaways per component**

1. **Price** is always the biggest lever. 1% of spend is the unit to think in.
2. **Payment terms:** with 8% interest, **+30 days = 0.67% of price in every case**
   (`spend · r · Δdays / 360`). It runs both ways: going from the 60-day reference down to 14 days
   costs us 1.0% of spend. **Exchange rate: 1% price ≈ 45 payment days.** For a **cash-short supplier,
   faster payment is worth more to it than to us** (its margin counts payment "at what money is worth
   to it now"). That's the cheapest trade in the game.
3. **Freight** (EXW/FCA/DAP, € per unit from the case) is **not negotiable in any practice case**;
   it's fixed. It may appear in hidden cases: check `negotiable` before spending a move on it.
   DAP costs us €0 freight, FCA about €1.90 per unit in P01.
4. **Quality:** `committed · ppm / 1e6 · (cost_per_defect − chargeback)`. Small for most cases,
   **dominant in quality cases and P10**. A chargeback ≥ cost_per_defect wipes the quality cost to zero.
   Watch the must-haves (P11: ppm ≤ 1500).
5. **Relationship** (`stabilise` / `secure_supply` only): `€/pt · soft(sustainable_margin − supplier_margin) · years`,
   with soft = g²/10 up to 5 pts. **Gentle at first and then expensive**: squeezing a strategic supplier
   2 pts below its sustainable margin costs €6k in P01, 5 pts costs €39k (≈ 3.75% of price).
   **Never squeeze stabilise/secure_supply suppliers below their cost floor.** Use should-cost to
   estimate where that floor is.
6. **Lead time / uncovered need:** in **capacity cases** late_cost_per_day (€1,800–2,400 per day) and
   fallback (+25%) beat price. **Secure supply first, then price.**

## 1b. Category goals: what each one means and which suppliers get which

The goal comes in every case (`buyer.category_goal`) and **sets the rates**, so it changes what a
"good deal" is. From the 19 practice cases:

| goal | cases (supplier, category) | fallback risk | index risk | relationship charge | what wins |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **stabilise** (strategic) | P01 70110 castings, P02 70510 electrical, P03 70530 electrical, P06 70120/70150 castings, P14 70560 electrical, P17 70550 electrical | 10% | 4% | **yes**: €4k–32k per margin point per year, sustainable margin 0.5–8% | fair price near cost-justified, **keep the supplier healthy**, stable terms; don't squeeze below its margin; avoid index clauses (4% risk) unless the index path favours us |
| **secure_supply** (bottleneck) | P09 70220 machined, P10 70410 seals | **25%** | 3% | **yes** | **getting the volume and lead time first** (late €1.8–2.4k per day, uncovered need +25%); commitment/contract length buys capacity; price second |
| **quality_first** | P11 70140 castings (red), P12 70310 sheet metal (red) | 8% | 2% | no | **ppm commitment + chargebacks** (must-have ppm ≤ 1500 in P11); price third |
| **leverage** | P05 machined RFQ, P08 70710 / P16 70720 / P18 70740 raw material, P13 70230 machined | **3%** | 1% | no | **push price hard**: competition, rival quotes, index clauses are cheap (1% risk); walking away is cheap (fallback +3%) |
| **routine** | P04 70620 / P07 70610 / P19 70640 C-parts, P15 70430 seals bundle | 5% | 2% | no | small money: quick, simple deal; contract length/bundle for price; don't spend many rounds |

**Predicting the goal for hidden suppliers** (same generator, other suppliers): it follows the
**category and the situation**:
- castings and electrical (A-class, single source, high spend) → **stabilise**
- seals and machined parts with capacity/allocation signals (full order book, "cannot confirm your forecast") → **secure_supply**
- red scorecard + escalation letter → **quality_first**
- raw material and multi-source machined parts → **leverage**
- C-parts, fasteners and small bundles → **routine**

The case states its goal, but the dossier tells you **why**. E.g. for a stabilise supplier that is
cash-short (70130, 70150) the relationship charge bites hardest, so payment-for-price is the trade.

**Per-goal playbook in one line each:**
- **stabilise:** "fair and stable". Cost-justified price, longer contract, effective date later, faster payment if they're cash-short.
- **secure_supply:** "volume first". Cover the full need, meet the lead-time must-have, then negotiate price with commitment.
- **quality_first:** "commit to quality". Tight ppm + chargeback ≥ cost_per_defect, an 8D plan, price only after.
- **leverage:** "hard bargain". Rival quotes, index evidence, an index clause, a credible walk-away.
- **routine:** "fast and clean". 2–3 rounds, accept a decent deal, multi-year for a discount.

## 2. Negotiation history: suppliers always settle 1-4 points below what they ask

`negotiation_history.csv`, 225 settled renewals (2023-2025):

- **gap = asked − settled is between 1.0 and 4.0 percentage points in all 225 events**: mean 2.47,
  roughly uniform (look at the histogram), independent of the size of the ask.
- A linear fit `settled = −1.92 + 0.77·asked + 0.23·cost_driver_change_12m` has **R² = 0.93**.
  Asks *and* settlements track the weighted cost-driver indices (asked ~ +2.4 pts above the driver
  change, settled ~ on it: mean −0.10).
- **Rule of thumb for targets:** settle at about **the cost-justified change** (driver-weighted index move)
  and no worse than **ask − 2.5 pts**. A good negotiator beats the 4-point maximum.
- 20 events carry the note *"accepted below full cost to keep the volume"* (sup 70110, 70120, 70130,
  70160, 70550): these suppliers **have already gone below cost once**. They're under margin pressure, so
  in a stabilise case the relationship charge bites quickly.

Supplier features vs concession (Spearman, n = 46; small sample, read as hints):

| Larger concession (gap) when… | ρ |
|---|---|
| we are a big share of its turnover (`our_share_of_their_turnover`) | +0.34 |
| we paid late more often (it wants its cash: the trade is cash for price) | +0.31 / +0.35 |
| credit index worse (higher) | +0.23 |
| ppm high (weak quality) | +0.20 |
| **smaller** concession for large groups (staff, turnover) | −0.30 / −0.23 |

By owner type: **groups ask the most (+2.5%) and settle the highest (+0.1%)**. Family/founder firms
ask about +1% and settle about −1.5%. Groups have pricing committees and price freezes (section 5):
**on groups negotiate terms, not price.**

## 3. Price-increase letters vs cost-justified change. The core argument.

Weighted cost-driver change over 12 months (`cost_drivers.weight × index change`), weights summing to
about 0.67 (the rest is margin and overhead):

| letter | case | asks | cost-justified | unjustified | notice given vs contract |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| doc:20260911-70110-P01 | P01 | +9.5% | +2.3% | **+7.2 pts** | **51 d vs 90 d (too short)** |
| doc:20260914-70510-P02 | P02 | +6.5% | +11.2% | −4.7 (justified! copper +47.7%) | 109 d ok |
| doc:20260919-70550-P17 | P17 | +8.0% | +8.0% | 0.0 (electronic components +13.5%) | 104 d ok |
| doc:20260927-70230-P13 | P13 | +7.0% | +4.7% | +2.3 pts (cold-drawn bar +12.3%) | **35 d vs 90 d (too short)** |

- **P01** is the textbook case: the ask is about 4× the cost justification, notice is too short (an
  effective date of 2027-01-01 or later is defensible), the owner is *"data-naive"* (own spreadsheet)
  and *"expects partnership"*. → Polite, data-led e-mail: energy −3.7% (PPI-D351), iron casting +1.8%,
  labour +2.7%, plus the notice-period argument. Target about +2–3% from 2027-01-01.
- **P02** (copper motor) is **justified**: pushing on price gets "not our reality". Better levers:
  an **index clause** on CU-NOTATION (shifts risk; check `index_risk_pct` and `index_paths`) and payment days.
- **P17** is exactly justified: don't fight the index; trade volume and payment instead.

Index moves over 12 months to the latest month (`index_values.csv`), the arguments that matter:

| falls / flat (use against increases) | rises (expect pushback) |
|---|---|
| Electricity generation PPI-D351 **−3.7%** (−43% vs 2022 peak) | Loaded electronic boards PPI-C2612 **+40.8%** |
| Iron ore WB-FEORE −5.4% | Copper WB-CU **+45%**, CU-NOTATION +47.7% |
| Bearings PPI-C2815 +0.8% | Aluminium +23.7%, Nat. gas EU +128.6%, Day-ahead power +73.4% |
| Iron casting PPI-C2451 +1.8%, fasteners +1.7%, motors +1.6% | Electronic components +13.5%, cold-drawn bar +12.3% |
| Labour LCI-C +2.7% | Steel PPI-C241 +6.5% |

Careful: **power is down vs 2022 and vs 12 months ago on the PPI, but up on day-ahead spot and gas**.
Choose the series and window honestly. A supplier with a hedged energy contract will answer with the
spot series. Citation rule: two `idx` IDs, same series, ≤ 12 months apart, ending within the last
12 months of data.

## 4. Should-cost vs current price (`should_cost.csv`, ±5–15% error, **no margin included**)

- Most castings and electrical parts are **priced at or below should-cost** (P01 casing −4.6%,
  P02 −7.1%, P17 −7.8%). These suppliers are **thin or negative on margin**, so squeezing price
  triggers the relationship charge and "I cannot sign anything that lowers our margin".
- **Fat margins (price ≫ should-cost) = room to push:** P10 seals **+23.0%** (sup:70410), 70420 seals
  +16.6%, 70110's other casing +10.3%, 70440 +8.1%.
- Use `cost:` IDs as evidence only where price > cost. Citing a cost above the price helps them.

## 6. Other levers and signals

- **Payment behaviour (we are the buyer):** we pay **late on ~80–94% of invoices** for most
  suppliers (mean +3.6 d, up to +40 d; worst: 70580 +19 d, 70295, 70250, 70290, 70340). Offering
  *reliable* payment is a cheap concession that suppliers value.
- **RFQs (`quotes.csv`):** spread within an RFQ is a median of 3.3% (max 42.6%). **The awarded quote is
  the cheapest in only about half the cases**; lead time and terms matter. 16 past quotes omit
  `validity/incoterm/payment_days`, and **unstated terms default to the supplier's standard terms**,
  so always state every negotiable field.
- **Contracts:** every framework agreement fixes prices per calendar year with **90 days' notice**
  (70420: 180 d). Only 4 have index clauses. That makes the **notice argument** strong for P01 (51 d)
  and P13 (35 d).
- **Scorecards:** red: 70140 (P11, 2376 ppm), 70150, 70170 (5682 ppm), 70310 (P12, 4118 ppm), 70350
  (5556 ppm, 53.8% OTD). Cite `sc:` for the OTD % or ppm; that's checked.
- **Concentration:** we are 11.3% of 70310's turnover, 8.2% of 70330, 7.1% of 70190, 7.0% of 70140.
  High share = we matter to them = leverage (and responsibility in stabilise cases).

## 8. What this means for the agent

1. **Before the first e-mail**, compute from data: the cost-justified change, should-cost margin,
   notice-period check, motives, concession profile and the € value of each lever (section 1).
   That's datasets A/B/C plus `arena_kit.total_cost`.
2. **Pick the lever by motive, not by habit**: cash-short → payment-for-price; price frozen or a group
   → terms; needs volume → volume/contract length; hedged → index clause or timing; capacity-tight →
   commitment; data-naive → index evidence.
3. **Target** about the cost-justified change, never worse than ask − 2.5 pts. In stabilise and
   secure_supply cases, stay above the supplier's sustainable margin.
4. **One argument per sentence, max 3 checked per e-mail, only pre-verified claims (dataset C).**
5. **Offer 3 term sheets** that trade levers against each other (e.g. lower price + faster payment /
   current price + later effective date / middle).
